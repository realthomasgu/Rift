Rift guide

The desktop

The desktop is two bars and the windows between them. The top bar has the Applications button at the left, the clock in the middle and the status icons at the right. The bar along the bottom is the dock.

Starting an app

Press Super and space, or click Applications. The menu lists what is installed, by section, and typing filters the list. The arrow keys walk it, Enter starts what is selected, and Esc clears the field and then closes the menu.

Over the apps is Places: your home folder, the folders in it, and the exchange partition when your drive has one. Click one and it opens in Files.

The field is not only a list of apps. It also takes commands for the system, such as wifi off or volume 40, Nushell pipelines, and questions in plain language for the assistant.

Plain words that are none of those look through your home folder as well. Type what a file was about, wait a moment, and the files closest to it in meaning come up under the field with the folder each one is in: search for teeth checkup booking and the letter from the dentist is there, whether those words are in it or not. Click one and it opens in the app for its kind. Enter still asks the assistant. That needs the index of your home folder, which the Search page in Settings makes and keeps up to date; until there is one, the field says so in a line under the field.

Super and H puts into the field what you say instead of what you type. The menu opens and the shell listens until you press the same keys again, and then the words go in the field and Enter follows them. The assistant has the rest of it.

The dock

The dock holds the apps you have pinned first, then the ones that are running. A click starts an app or brings its window forward, a middle click opens another window, and a right click gives you its windows by name, a new window, pinning, and close. The workspaces are the numbered buttons at the right.

Before them stand the trash, while there is something in it, and every disk you have mounted. A click opens either one in Files, and a right click on a disk ejects it. They are there while there is something to open and gone when there is not, the same as a running app.

The clock and the status icons

The clock opens a calendar with the notifications you have had, and a switch for Do not disturb. The status icons open the system menu: sound, screen brightness, wired and wireless networks, Bluetooth, the battery, and rows that lock the screen, log out, restart or shut down.

Windows

Windows are laid out as columns on a strip that scrolls sideways, one strip per workspace. A new window opens as a column of its own next to the one you were in, and nothing overlaps unless you float it. Super and the left and right arrows walk the columns; Super and the up and down arrows walk the windows inside a column.

The terminal on Super and the key above Tab drops down over the desktop from the top of the screen and goes away again with the same key.

What was open

The desktop writes down what is open as you work: for each window the app that opened it, the workspace and the screen it is on, and where it stands in the layout. It is kept with your files rather than with the machine, so it goes where the drive goes. rift session in a terminal prints it.

At the first login after the machine starts, those apps open again: each one on the workspace it was on and in the column it stood in, one after another, so the desktop comes back as you left it on whatever machine the drive is in. Windows are not the size they were, because the screen is a different size. An app the machine you are on does not have is passed over, and a notification at the end says which ones. An app that keeps all its windows in one process, the terminal among them, comes back with one window even when it had several, and the notification says so at the time. A shell that starts again in the middle of a session brings nothing back, so a crash does not open everything twice. Settings, Owner, Bring the session back turns it off.

Pictures of the screen

Print takes a screenshot of a part of the screen you draw with the pointer, Ctrl and Print takes the whole screen, and Alt and Print takes the window you are in. They are saved in Pictures.

Ctrl, Alt, Shift and R starts recording the screen, and the same keys stop it. While a recording is running the bar shows a round mark next to the status icons, and when it stops a notification names the file. Recordings are saved in Videos, as MP4, without sound.
